Whose word?

Anyone who holds a token and wants to be believed when they say they'll keep holding it.

  • The one who launched it

    “The team's tokens stay put for the first week.”

  • The one who holds a lot

    “I'm not selling into this. Watch my wallet.”

  • The one who was paid in it

    “I got these for the post. I'm holding them for a month.”

How it works

  1. Put IMD on it

    Pick a token, an amount and a deadline, and lock IMD behind one sentence: my wallet won't move more than this before then, or I'll send at least this much to them by then. Name someone, and a broken bond goes to them.

  2. Time runs out

    You close the promise at the deadline, or anyone does two minutes later. That fixes the exact block range it covered, start to finish.

  3. The swarm counts

    A few seconds later, the contract pays the IdentityMD oracle 0.5 IMD out of the bond. A panel of agents sums the wallet's transfers over those blocks and signs the number.

  4. The number decides

    Kept: the bond goes home, less 1% for PINKY stakers. Broken: it goes to the person you named, less the same 1%. Named nobody? A tenth to the first watcher who asked, the rest between PINKY stakers and a burn.

A promise covers one wallet and one ordinary token (ERC-20; NFTs come soon), and nobody can pause or edit it once it's made. If the oracle can't answer after three tries, the bond is returned.

Before launch the IdentityMD swarm audited the contracts: one audit of the code as written, four specialist audits and a judge. It did the same for the second vault. What they found was fixed, and the list is public, as are the swarm's own records of the jobs (first, second) and the code exactly as it was deployed (first, second). That is a review by AI agents, not an audit by an outside firm, so read the contracts before you trust them.

Questions people ask

Is this a locker?

No. A locker takes your tokens away. Pinky never touches them: they stay in your wallet and you can sell any minute. What you lock is IMD, and selling costs you that.

What counts as moving?

Every transfer of that token out of the wallet: a sale, a send to your other wallet, a deposit into staking or a pool. Tokens coming in don't count.

What's a promise to pay?

A promise the other way round: I'll send at least this much of this token to this address by then. The oracle sums what actually reached them from your wallet. Short of it, and the bond goes to them instead. Good for a deal: a payment you owe, tokens you said you'd hand out, a buyback you announced.

What does "make it to someone" mean?

You name an address. If you break your word, your bond goes to them, less 1%, instead of being split between a watcher, stakers and a burn. A promise to pay is made to the person you pay unless you name someone else.

What if I sell from another wallet?

Then the promise doesn't see it. A promise covers one wallet, the one that made it. That's why the board says whose wallet it is and how much it holds.

Who decides whether I kept it?

The IdentityMD oracle. A panel of agents sums every transfer out of the wallet over the exact blocks the promise covered, and the contract only accepts a number the oracle has signed. Nobody here can overrule it.

What does it cost?

One oracle question, 0.5 IMD, taken from the bond when someone asks, and 1% of what's left when it goes home, which goes to PINKY stakers. Plus gas on Robinhood Chain for your signatures.

Can I cancel or change a promise?

No. Not you, not us. Once it's made, it runs until the deadline, and it keeps counting until someone closes it, so close it yourself when the time is up: you can from the very second, everyone else two minutes later.

What if the oracle doesn't answer?

If the panel can't agree, the oracle says so and anyone can ask again right away. If it just stays quiet, anyone can ask again after a day. Three tries in all; after that, or a week after closing, the rest of the bond goes back to you.

Who can change the contracts?

Nobody can change the code. The vault's owner can point it at another oracle signer or Intake, which is a real trust point: the owner is 0x89d6…f8ef, the team's wallet. In the second vault any such change is announced on chain and takes effect only after seven days. Staking has no owner at all.

Does it work for NFTs?

Soon. The second vault already knows how to count NFT transfers and to check what a wallet holds at the deadline. We're switching those on once the oracle has answered one of each for real. Until then the form refuses an NFT address, so nobody gets a promise that can't be judged.
